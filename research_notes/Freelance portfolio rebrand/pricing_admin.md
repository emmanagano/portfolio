# Pricing, Packaging, Payments and Legal/Admin Basics for a Philippines-Based Freelance Web Developer (US/AU/UK clients)

Research date: 2026-11-19. Method note: WebFetch could not reach most hosts in this session (upwork.com, payoneer.com and sweldoph.com failed DNS resolution through the session proxy). Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from full page reads. The numbers are dated where the source gives a date. Treat vendor blogs (Wise, Seahawk, Second Talent, etc.) as marketing-sourced. Before publishing any figure, verify it against the live page.

## 1. Typical rates (hourly and per project), 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Onshore freelance web developers in the US, UK and AU typically charge about US$50–100+/hr, £30–80/hr (£250–600/day) and A$90–150/hr. Philippines-based freelancers serving those markets mostly charge US$12–40/hr (mid-level React around $25–40), and senior specialists reach $60–100. Per project, a 5-page marketing site from a freelancer usually costs US$1,500–5,000. A template-based small Shopify store costs about A$2,000–8,000 (roughly US$1,300–5,300). Care/maintenance plans run US$29–300/month per site for typical small-business tiers.

## 2. Pricing models: hourly vs fixed vs productized vs value-based

### Takeaway
Productized fixed-price packages are well suited to repeatable small-business work. A package has a defined scope, a set number of revisions, a timeline, and a deposit to book. They are easy for international clients to buy and protect the freelancer from hourly undercutting. Hourly billing is best kept for open-ended add-on work and care-plan overages. No good sources were found on value-based pricing for freelancers in this niche.

### Cited Findings
- A productized service is "a predefined, packaged service that's sold like a physical good with a fixed price" — [Shopify: productized service](https://www.shopify.com/blog/productized-service)
- Twine describes productization as a repeatable service with a defined outcome, scope, price, timeline and intake process. Its example is a **$2,500 fixed fee with a 50% deposit to book**. It warns that scope stays "soft" without firm boundaries — [Twine: productized services for freelance designers](https://www.twine.net/blog/productized-services-for-freelance-designers/)
- Squarespace's package outline: package name, target audience, deliverables (page count, responsive design, SEO), price, and guidance on choosing a tier — [Squarespace Pros: web design packages](https://pros.squarespace.com/blog/web-design-packages)
- State what is and isn't included in each package, because clients may need customizations outside it — [Taskip: productized services](https://taskip.net/productized-services/); [Elegant Themes: pros and cons of packages](https://elegantthemes.com/blog/tips-tricks/3-reasons-to-offer-web-design-packages-and-3-reasons-not-to) (2015, dated)
- ManyPixels (vendor) lists "unlimited web design" subscriptions at **$500–3,000** — [ManyPixels](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/web-design/website-design-packages)
- For care plans, flat pricing is simplest for new providers. Its drawback is that a brochure site and a store shouldn't cost the same, so tiers are common — [Codeable](https://www.codeable.io/blog/wordpress-maintenance-pricing/)
- A care plan typically includes core/plugin/theme updates, backups, uptime monitoring, security, a monthly report and a support channel. Higher tiers add faster response, developer hours, and SEO/performance work — [Pantheon](https://pantheon.io/learning-center/wordpress/maintenance-plans); [Seahawk](https://seahawkmedia.com/wordpress/wordpress-maintenance-packages/)
- Freelancer risk in maintenance: availability. If the freelancer is away when a site goes down, the client waits — [Freshy Sites](https://freshysites.com/blog/how-much-should-you-pay-for-wordpress-maintenance-services-a-pricing-guide/)

### Inferences
- A sensible ladder for a new freelancer:
  - **Starter** (1–3 page landing/portfolio site)
  - **Business** (5-page marketing site with CMS/blog)
  - **Store** (small Shopify/WooCommerce build)
  - **Care plans** in 2–3 tiers, with Basic and Plus/Store variants (Plus includes some edit hours)
  - Hourly or "change request" pricing for anything outside scope
- Publishing "starting at" prices on the portfolio filters out low-budget leads and suits async international buyers who compare options without a call.
- Recurring care plans smooth the income from one-off builds. Bundling the first 1–3 months of care into the build price makes it easy for the client to start.

### Gaps
- No credible source found on value-based pricing for small-business web projects, or on how well it works for new freelancers.

## 3. Deposits, milestones, scope-creep controls and contracts

### Takeaway
The standard is a **50% non-refundable (or clearly defined) deposit, with 50% due before launch**. Larger projects often use **50/25/25** (deposit / design approval / pre-launch). Contracts should cover deliverables, exclusions, a fixed number of revision rounds, change orders for extra work, a kill fee (25–50%), and IP transfer only on final payment. Of the named template tools, AND.CO (Fiverr Workspace) shut down on 1 March 2026 and Docracy is unverified/likely defunct, so Bonsai or alternatives are the remaining options.

### Cited Findings
- "The standard for web design is 50% upfront, 50% on delivery," with 50/25/25 for larger projects (50% upfront, 25% at design approval, 25% before go-live) — [Contra: web design payment schedules](https://contra.com/p/NAAvZWNm-web-design-payment-schedules-a-guide-to-deposits-and-milestones)
- A clause saying only "50% upfront" invites disputes. State whether the deposit is non-refundable, partly refundable, or credited against the total — [Sprintlaw AU: web design payment milestones](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/web-design-payment-milestones/); [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/web-design-milestone-payments/)
- Kill fee: a percentage of the total, or payment for work done to date. Templates suggest **25–50% of the remaining balance**, plus holding work-in-progress files until paid — [HubSpot: web design contract](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/web-design-contract); [SitePoint: bulletproof web design contract](https://www.sitepoint.com/bulletproof-web-design-contract/)
- Specify revision rounds (e.g. **two rounds per deliverable**). Out-of-scope changes require a written change order and additional fee. List exclusions such as logo design, copywriting and SEO — [Elementor: web design contract](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/); [WP Engine/Flywheel: defining your contract's scope](https://getflywheel.com/layout/defining-your-contracts-scope/)
- The Australian Consumer Law can override unfair deposit or kill-fee terms, so enforceability varies by client jurisdiction — [Sprintlaw AU: refund and cancellation terms](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/refund-and-cancellation-terms-for-web-design-agencies-in-australia/)
- **AND.CO / Fiverr Workspace:** Fiverr help-centre pages (FR/DE/ES/NL) say it ceases operations on **1 March 2026**, with last billing in January 2026. A migration guide says data is deleted after cutoff — [Fiverr help: Fiverr Workspace](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/fr/articles/4410092851345-Fiverr-Workspace); [Plutio: AND.CO shutting down](https://www.plutio.com/blog/and-co-shutting-down-migration-guide). The English help page has softer wording — [Fiverr help (EN)](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410092851345-Fiverr-Workspace)

### Inferences
- Recommended clauses for international clients:
  - Scope and deliverables, with an exclusions list
  - Client responsibilities and content deadlines, plus a pause/restart fee if content is late
  - Revision rounds, then an hourly rate or change order
  - Payment schedule in USD/AUD/GBP, with late fees and work pausing on non-payment
  - Kill fee
  - IP/ownership transfers on final payment, with the freelancer keeping portfolio rights
  - Third-party licences (themes, plugins, fonts) billed to the client
  - Hosting and credentials ownership (client-owned accounts)
  - Warranty period (e.g. 14–30 days of bug fixes)
  - Limitation of liability
  - Governing law and dispute venue
  - Electronic signatures
- Collecting the deposit before starting work is the single strongest protection against non-payment from overseas clients, since cross-border debt collection is impractical.

### Gaps
- Bonsai's current pricing and template set were not retrieved (search returned nothing).
- No source confirmed Docracy's current status. It is believed defunct, but this is unverified, so don't recommend it.
- Philippine-law specifics on governing law and e-signature validity for cross-border contracts were not researched.

## 4. How international clients pay Philippines freelancers

### Takeaway
**Wise** is the cheapest way to receive USD/GBP/AUD via local account details, and PH users pay a one-time ₱1,400 to unlock account details. **Payoneer** works and many marketplaces support it, but it layers fees: up to 1% on receipts, a $29.95 annual fee if receiving under $6k/year, and up to 3.99% on card payments. **PayPal** is easiest for clients but most expensive: about 4.4% plus a fixed fee to receive internationally, plus 3–4% FX conversion. **Stripe** does not directly support Philippine-registered businesses per available sources, so card checkout is best handled through Payoneer/PayPal invoices or a PH gateway.

### Cited Findings
- **Wise:** receive USD, EUR, GBP, SGD and PHP for free via local account details. **One-time ₱1,400** fee to unlock full account features, no monthly fee. Small fixed fees apply for SWIFT receipts (Wise's own blog, so competitor-biased) — [Wise PH: Wise vs Payoneer](https://wise.com/ph/blog/wise-vs-payoneer); [Wise PH: international payment methods](https://wise.com/ph/blog/international-payment-methods). Note: one search summary attributed the ₱1,400 fee to Payoneer, but Wise PH pages attribute it to Wise. Treat it as Wise's fee and verify.
- **Payoneer:** **up to 1%** fee on receipts via local account details. **$29.95 annual fee** if receiving under US$6,000/yr. **₱50** to withdraw under ₱7,000 to a local bank. **Up to 3.99%** for credit card payments. No PHP balance, but PHP withdrawals are allowed (figures via Wise's comparison pages) — [Wise PH: Payoneer fees](https://wise.com/ph/blog/payoneer-fees); [Payoneer: fees for Filipinos](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/country-guides/payoneer-fees-and-costs-for-filipinos/) (could not be fetched)
- **PayPal (PH):**
  - Receiving: **4.40% + fixed fee** for international receipts; 3.40% + ₱15 domestic
  - Withdrawing: **₱50** to a PH bank under ₱7,000 (free above that); **free** to GCash; ₱250 to Maya
  - Currency conversion: **3–4%**
  These are third-party figures — [Wise PH: receive money on PayPal](https://wise.com/ph/blog/receive-money-paypal-philippines); [Lilach Bullock: PayPal in Philippines](https://www.lilachbullock.com/paypal-in-philippines/)
- **Stripe:** "at the time of writing, Stripe doesn't directly support businesses incorporated in the Philippines" — [Wise PH: Stripe vs PayPal](https://wise.com/ph/blog/stripe-vs-paypal). Stripe's own guide says merchant-account availability for local PH entities "remains subject to regional onboarding rollout" — [Stripe: payments in the Philippines](https://stripe.com/resources/more/payments-in-the-philippines)
- On the sending side, Wise quotes a sample fee of **US$4.69 + 0.6%** for a USD→PHP transfer (sample quote; live pricing varies) — [Wise US: send money to Philippines](https://wise.com/us/send-money/send-money-to-philippines)

### Inferences
- Practical setup:
  - Invoice in the client's currency. Give US clients Wise USD account details (ACH/wire) and UK/AU clients GBP/AUD details.
  - Offer PayPal or a Payoneer payment request as a card option, and pass on or price in the ~4–5% fee.
  - Convert to PHP in Wise and withdraw to a PH bank/GCash.
- Invoicing tools: Wise and Payoneer both have built-in payment requests. A dedicated invoicing app (Bonsai or alternatives) adds contracts and proposals. BIR-compliant invoice requirements are separate (see Q5).

### Gaps
- Official current Wise "receive" fees for USD wire/ACH to PH users could not be fetched.
- PayPal's official PH fee page was not retrieved.
- Stripe's status could not be confirmed from a 2026 Stripe primary source.
- No source checked whether Stripe Atlas (US LLC) is a viable workaround for a PH resident. Not researched.

## 5. Philippine tax and registration for freelancers with foreign income

### Takeaway
A PH-resident freelancer must register with the BIR as self-employed, is taxed on worldwide income, and must issue **invoices, not official receipts** (under the EOPT Act, ORs issued after 30 June 2024 don't count). With gross receipts at or below ₱3M and no VAT registration, they can elect the **8% tax on gross receipts above ₱250,000** in lieu of graduated rates and the 3% percentage tax. The ₱500 annual registration fee was abolished from 22 Jan 2024. The mandatory e-invoicing deadline of 31 Dec 2026 (RR 11-2025 as amended by RR 26-2025, with RMC 98-2026 guidelines) appears to exempt micro taxpayers.

### Cited Findings
- **8% option:**
  - Available to self-employed individuals and professionals with gross sales/receipts **≤ ₱3M** who are not VAT-registered, in lieu of graduated rates and the 3% percentage tax.
  - Elected on the first quarterly return (**BIR Form 1701Q**) and generally irrevocable for that year.
  - Most sources say the ₱250,000 is deducted first (8% of gross receipts plus other non-operating income above ₱250k). One Respicio piece says "from the first peso". That only applies to mixed-income earners, whose compensation income is taxed separately, so treat it as an outlier for pure freelancers.

  Sources: [Wise PH: freelance tax Philippines](https://wise.com/ph/blog/freelance-tax-philippines); [Respicio: freelancer tax filing](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/freelancer-tax-filing-in-the-philippines); [Beancount: BIR freelancer tax 2026 guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/philippines-bir-freelancer-influencer-tax-2026-guide); [Globe: freelance tax ITR](https://www.globe.com.ph/go/career-money/article/freelance-tax-philippines-how-to-file-itr.html)
- **Annual registration fee abolished:** under the EOPT Act (RA 11976), which amended NIRC Sec. 236, the BIR stopped collecting the ₱500 ARF from **22 January 2024** (BIR Advisory 8 Jan 2024; RMC 14-2024) — [PwC PH Tax Alert No. 2 (2024)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-02.html); [Manila Bulletin, 9 Jan 2024](https://mb.com.ph/2024/1/9/bir-scraps-p500-annual-registration-fee-for-business-taxpayers); [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/philippines-annual-registration-fee-will-not-be-collected-starting-22-january-2024)
- **Registration steps (2026 guide):**
  - Register as self-employed.
  - Pay a **₱30** documentary stamp tax.
  - Books of accounts (journal, ledger, cash receipts/disbursements) are still required and must be registered with the BIR. Micro-scale requirements are simpler.

  Sources: [PhilstarLife: how to register as a freelancer with BIR (2026)](https://philstarlife.com/self/848990-how-to-register-freelancer-bir-philippines-2026); [Respicio: freelancer BIR registration requirements](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/freelancer-bir-registration-requirements-in-the-philippines)
- **Invoices under EOPT:**
  - The invoice is now the single primary document for both goods and services, and the OR is only supplementary.
  - Sales of services moved from a cash to an accrual basis for VAT.
  - An "Official Receipt" issued after **30 June 2024** is treated as failure to issue an invoice. Unused ORs could be converted by striking out "Official Receipt" and stamping "Invoice".
  - Governing issuances: RR 3-2024, RR 7-2024, RR 11-2024, RMC 77-2024.

  Sources: [PwC PH: EoPT invoicing clarified (2024)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2024/eopt-invoicing-clarified.html); [Forvis Mazars: transitory provisions](https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/transitory-provisions-of-eopt-act-issuances); [KPMG PH July 2024: registration and invoicing update](https://kpmg.com/ph/en/home/insights/2024/07/compliance-an-update-to-registration-procedures-and-invoicing-requirements.html); [Respicio: what document freelancers should issue](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/what-document-should-freelancers-or-service-providers-issue-for-sales-of-services)
- **Zero-rated or exempt sales:** invoices must show the VAT breakdown or the classification of the sale as VAT-exempt or zero-rated. Since RR 3-2023, prior application for zero-rating is no longer necessary, but documentation must still support it — [Forvis Mazars: VAT invoicing requirements under EOPT](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/ease-of-paying-taxes-act/vat-invoicing-requirements-eopt-act); [Forvis Mazars: RMC 80-2023](https://forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/tax-alerts/rmc-no.-80-2023)
- **E-invoicing (2025–2026 update):**
  - RR 11-2025 set the mandatory e-invoicing (EIS) rules.
  - **RR 26-2025** moved the compliance deadline from 14 March 2026 to **31 December 2026**. Sources differ on its date: PwC says 16 Oct 2025, Orbitax says 5 Sep 2025.
  - **RMC 98-2026** (22 Sept 2026) issued implementing guidelines and kept the 31 Dec 2026 deadline.
  - Covered taxpayers: e-commerce/internet-transaction taxpayers classified **Small, Medium or Large (Micro taxpayers exempted)**, LTS/large taxpayers, and CAS/invoicing-software users.
  - Exporters and POS users follow later. Electronic sales reporting is separate and not yet activated.

  Sources: [PwC PH Tax Alert No. 29 (RR 26-2025)](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2025/tax-alert-29.html); [Grant Thornton PH: BIR e-invoicing guidelines retain 31 Dec 2026 deadline](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2026/bir-issues-guidelines-on-electronic-invoicing-retains-31-dec-2026-deadline/); [Daily Tribune, 23 Sep 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/23/bir-sets-31-december-e-invoicing-deadline); [KPMG US TaxNewsFlash Sep 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/09/philippines-e-invoicing-required-dec-31-2026.html); [Lookuptax: RR 11-2025](https://lookuptax.com/tax-changes/philippines/rr11-2025-eis-mandate)
- **Export services and RA 12066:** RA 12066 (CREATE MORE) zero-rates sales to export-oriented enterprises meeting a 70% export threshold. This concerns suppliers to registered exporters, not directly a freelancer's foreign clients — [search summary of PwC/Forvis sources above](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2024/compliance-change-on-invoicing-under-eopt.html)

### Inferences
- A new freelancer earning under ₱3M/yr (about US$51k at ~₱58/US$, rate unverified) would typically:
  - Register as self-employed (Form 1901, per general practice).
  - Elect 8% on the first 1701Q.
  - Issue BIR-registered invoices for each client payment.
  - Keep registered books (manual or loose-leaf).
  - File quarterly 1701Q and annual 1701A.
- Worked example: ₱1.2M gross receipts → 8% × (₱1.2M − ₱250k) = **₱76,000** tax, about 6.3% effective.
- As a micro taxpayer (under ₱3M gross sales, the EOPT classification), the freelancer appears **exempt from the 31 Dec 2026 e-invoicing mandate** even though income arrives via the internet. Confirm with RMC 98-2026 or a tax adviser.
- Exceeding ₱3M triggers mandatory VAT registration and loses 8% eligibility. Services rendered to foreign clients paid in foreign currency may then qualify for 0% VAT, but that needs adviser confirmation.
- Filing deadlines commonly cited under TRAIN: 1701Q due May 15, Aug 15 and Nov 15; 1701A due April 15. These dates come from general knowledge, not retrieved this session, so verify.
- Tax-filing apps (Taxumo, JuanTax) are commonly used by PH freelancers. No current pricing was retrieved.

## 6. Timezone and communication expectations

### Takeaway
PH time is fixed at UTC+8 with no daylight saving. **Australian clients overlap best** (AEST is 2 hours ahead of PH, giving about 6 hours of shared business day). **UK** is 7–8 hours behind, so a PH afternoon-to-evening shift covers UK mornings. **US** clients are 12–16 hours behind, so work is mostly async, plus a short early-morning or late-evening PH call window. Sources recommend a defined overlap window (about 2 hours for US), shared calendars, and noting PH public holidays.

### Cited Findings
- PH runs on PhST (UTC+8) and does not observe daylight saving — [Upwork: hire freelancers in the Philippines](https://www.upwork.com/hire/ph/)
- Australia: a 9–5 AEST schedule gives about 6 hours of overlap; AU is about 2 hours from Manila — [VirtualStaff.ph: assessing time zone overlap](https://www.virtualstaff.ph/blog/assess-right-time-zone-overlap-for-remote-team); [Cloudstaff: using time zones when outsourcing](https://www.cloudstaff.com/blog/general-outsourcing/how-to-use-time-zones-to-your-advantage-when-outsourcing/)
- UK: about 7–8 hours apart, with roughly 1 hour of natural overlap under standard hours, so a shifted start is usually needed — [VA Masters: time zone management](https://vamasters.com/time-zone-management-offshore-teams-guide/)
- US: a 12–16 hour gap, with essentially zero natural overlap for the West Coast under standard hours. A minimum 2-hour overlap window is recommended for US clients — [VA Masters: twelve-hour gap 2026](https://vamasters.com/virtual-assistant-time-zones-offshore-hiring-2026/); [HireTalent.ph: tools for EST schedules (Apr 2026)](https://www.hiretalent.ph/blog/tools-filipino-remote-workers-est-schedule)
- Recommended practices: don't expect perfect overlap; keep a shared holiday calendar (PH holidays differ); publish an availability calendar with overlap hours highlighted — [HireTalent.ph: dealing with time zones (Dec 2024)](https://hiretalent.ph/blog/dealing-with-time-zones-when-working-with-filipino-vas); [Freelancer.hk community article](https://www.freelancer.hk/community/articles/working-with-clients-in-different-time-zones)
- Source-quality note: some of these sources contradict each other on UK/US offsets (one cites an "8-hour" US Pacific difference). Treat their specific numbers with care. All are recruiter or outsourcing blogs.

### Inferences
- Exact offsets (computed, not sourced; verify with a converter since US/UK/AU observe DST):
  - **US Eastern** is 12 hours behind PH (13 in winter), and **Pacific** 15–16 hours behind. US 9am ET = 9–10pm PH.
  - **UK** is 7 hours behind PH in BST and 8 in GMT. UK 9am = 4–5pm PH.
  - **Sydney** is 2–3 hours ahead (AEDT in AU summer).
- A sensible default promise for small-business clients: replies within one business day, a fixed weekly call slot in the client's morning or evening, async updates via Loom/email, and a shared project board. Stating "based in the Philippines (UTC+8), overnight turnaround for US clients" can be framed as a benefit.
- AU clients are the easiest to serve live, so AU may be a good first target market.

### Gaps
- No survey data was found on client expectations for response times from offshore freelancers. The guidance above comes from VA and outsourcing vendor blogs.
