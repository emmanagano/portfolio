# Turn the hiring portfolio into a client-winning storefront

The rebrand should stop presenting a frontend developer looking for a job and start presenting a **one-person web studio for small, visually driven brands**: shops, studios, photographers and content creators who sell with pictures and stories. The site should sell **fixed-scope packages priced in USD/AUD/GBP**, lead with outcomes rather than the tech stack, and target **Australia first**, because the time zone overlap makes it easiest to serve. This matters because generic "I build websites" work is being squeezed from two sides. Template and WordPress gig volume has dropped by roughly 55–59% on Freelancer.com as owners switch to AI and no-code builders, and Philippine freelancers sit near the bottom of global rate tables. Competing on the deliverable or on price is a losing position. What AI builders and cheap bidders do not supply is the strategy, the copy, a brand-specific design, performance work, integrations and a clean launch. The existing projects and the agency web-support background already prove those skills, so the case studies should be rebuilt around them. One caveat runs through everything: the site will rarely generate leads by itself. Its job is to convert people who arrive from referrals, agency outreach, audit-style cold emails and marketplace proposals. That means the rebuild and the outreach plan have to be designed together. The admin side is manageable: Wise for receiving payments, a 50% deposit before any work starts, BIR registration with the 8% flat tax option, and invoices rather than official receipts.

## Sell an outcome to an audience cluster, not "Next.js developer"

Positioning experts agree that specializing reduces price competition. Philip Morgan argues that avoiding a "race to the bottom" means choosing a vertical or discipline ([WP Tonic](https://www.wp-tonic.com/?p=8804)). He also warns against positioning too early. He suggests about a year of experience first, focused on a problem domain rather than a platform ([100k Freelancing](https://medium.com/100k-freelancing/0118-positioning-and-professional-value-w-philip-morgan-b619f1f2ac3)). Jonathan Stark adds that moving from generalist to specialist is easier when you start from a skill than from a market ([Jonathan Stark](https://jonathanstark.com/daily/20230617-2359-skill-vs-market-specialization)). For a freelancer with one real paid client site, the answer is a **soft, testable focus**: a problem plus an audience, not a hard vertical lock-in. The three main projects already point to that audience. A fine-jewelry store, a travel/content site and a photographer's portfolio all serve **owner-run brands that sell through imagery and storytelling**. That gives a defensible cluster without betting everything on "photographers only."

A purely horizontal label is the weakest option. Payoneer notes that web and programming are among the most popular freelance fields but "don't pay the most due to a high supply of freelancers" ([Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/research-reports/average-freelance-salary-around-globe)). AI tools are shrinking the commodity end of that field. A Bluehost-commissioned survey found **53% of business owners had adopted AI website builders** instead of hiring developers ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/the-collapse-of-web-coding-gigs-is-getting-ugly-ai-takes-over-routine-website-development-jobs-and-business-owners-are-very-happy-with-its-job-so-far)). Freelancer.com data showed website design and PHP orders down about **59%**, WordPress about **58%** and HTML about **55%** ([hi-tech.ua](https://hi-tech.ua/en/ai-displaces-freelancers-wordpress-and-php-jobs-drop-by-nearly-60/)). Some commentators caution that the data does not show a total collapse ([PC Tech Magazine](https://pctechmag.com/2026/04/will-freelance-website-designers-survive-the-rise-of-no-code-and-ai-platforms/)), and the Bluehost survey is vendor-funded. The direction is still clear: the value is moving to strategy and results, not basic sites ([Roger Wong](https://rogerwong.me/2025/06/what-do-ai-site-builders-mean-for-freelancers)). The market has also matured. **83% of small businesses already have a website**, up from 64% in 2018 ([Clutch via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250821848152/en/Clutch-Report-No-Code-Tools-Fuel-Website-Growth-Yet-17-of-Small-Businesses-are-Still-Offline)). So redesigns, migrations, speed fixes and "rescue my DIY site" work form a bigger pool than first websites.

The current hero tagline, "Design. Write. Code.", lists activities. Copy guidance consistently favors the pattern "I help [audience] with [expensive problem]" ([Jonathan Stark PDF](https://jonathanstark.com/dl/best-of-positioning-for-consultants.pdf)), and Upwork reports that niche headlines with a stated benefit attract better-matched clients ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/freelancer-headlines)). Keep "Design. Write. Code." as a supporting capability line. Replace the `site.ts` `role` ("Frontend Developer") and `pitch` with something like "Websites for small brands that sell with pictures and stories — designed, written and built by one person," or "Fast, good-looking sites for small shops, studios and creators: copy, design and code in one hand-off." The end-to-end angle is the strongest differentiator. Copy does much of the converting on small-business sites ([Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/copywriting-for-small-business/)), and bundling brand, copy and design makes most sense for businesses that are starting fresh or rebranding ([Redefine Web](https://redefineweb.com/blog/small-business-branding-and-website-packages/)). The risk is that "I do everything" reads as generalist, so always attach the bundle to the audience.

**Keep the personal name.** A studio name can signal scale, but a solo person behind one can make clients "wonder what the freelancer is hiding" ([Better Proposals](https://betterproposals.io/blog/personal-vs-business-brand-freelance/)). Personal-name authority also "travels with you" ([Freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.com/blog/naming-freelance-business/)). The personal-name domain already has equity. Clutch presents a freelancer's edge over an agency as direct control and faster decisions ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/web-designers/resources/freelance-web-designer-vs-agency)), and a real face and voice deliver that. A business name can be added later for invoices and contracts ([Digital Deepak](https://blog.digitaldeepak.com/p/balancing-your-personal-brand-with)).

**Face the "cheap offshore" anchor directly rather than hiding the location.** A Remitly study of Upwork rates put the Philippines **7th lowest of 40 countries** ([Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2024/4/17/philippines-ranks-7th-for-lowest-freelancer-pay-globally)). One Filipino engineer who now charges US$60/hr wrote that each cheap bid "reinforced unfairly low pay as the norm" ([blog](https://astro-theme-cactus-llego.pages.dev/posts/filipino-software-engineer-advocating-fair-pay)). The evidence also shows a way out. Filipino freelancers with overseas clients earn **57% more per hour** than local-only peers ([Payoneer PH 2022](https://blog.payoneer.com/freelancers/philippines-freelance-market-2022-report)). The counter-signals are fixed-price packages, which remove hourly comparisons; detailed case studies; a written process; stated working hours; a response-time promise; and native-quality English copy. The copywriting itself proves that last point. Australia is the natural first market: AEST is only about 2 hours ahead of PH time, which gives roughly **6 hours of shared business day** ([VirtualStaff.ph](https://www.virtualstaff.ph/blog/assess-right-time-zone-overlap-for-remote-team)).

## Rebuild the homepage from résumé to sales page

Clients ask "what have you done for people like me?" and rarely ask for a résumé ([Millo](https://millo.co/freelancer-resume)). Everything on the current homepage that speaks to recruiters should therefore be removed or demoted. Freelancer-site guides converge on a blueprint ([selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/); [Wix](https://www.wix.com/blog/freelance-website-examples)): a niche value-proposition hero, services with "starting at" prices, three to six outcome-led case studies, testimonials, a short about section with a photo, and a prominent booking CTA. Studio sites usually add a numbered process ([Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/web-design-studio-website-examples/)). No controlled conversion data exists on section order, so the order below is a synthesis, not a tested benchmark. One finding shapes the priorities more than any other: a survey of 3,424 independents reportedly found only **4% of projects came through the freelancer's own website** ([Namecheap](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/why-freelancer-websites-fail-and-how-to-fix-yours/)). The figure is secondhand, but it fits every other source. Design the site for a referred or cold-pitched prospect doing a 30-second credibility check: a clear offer, fast proof and an easy next step.

| Current section (file) | Action | New role |
|---|---|---|
| Hero: "open to work" badge, résumé download (`site.openToWork`, `site.resumeUrl`) | Remove both. Move the résumé to an unlinked URL if job applications continue | Audience + outcome headline, photo, "Book a free project call" CTA, one-line proof (e.g. "Based in the Philippines, working Australian business hours") |
| — | **Add** after the hero | Services/packages with "starting at" prices in USD (AUD/GBP toggle optional) |
| SelectedWork (`projects.ts`) | Keep and reframe | Case studies: client → problem → what was built → results → quote → benefit-framed "Built with" line |
| — | **Add** | Process (Discovery → Copy → Design → Build → Launch → Care), then Testimonials |
| Challenges (`challenges.ts`) | Move off the homepage | "Lab" page linked from the footer or About. Gamer Sign-Up and Flatline show craft but not client value |
| Skills marquee (`skills.ts`) | Remove from the homepage | A short "Tools I use" line inside services and case studies |
| Experience timeline (`experience.ts`) | Collapse | One credibility paragraph on About: agency web support (DNS, migrations, launches), freelance builds |
| AboutTeaser | Strengthen | Photo, location and UTC+8 hours, response-time promise, "you work directly with me" |
| — | **Add** | FAQ: time zones, payment, who owns code/domain/hosting, post-launch support, editing content yourself |
| Contact (EmailJS) | Upgrade to a qualifying form | Project type, budget range, timeline, referral source, then a booking link |
| — | **Add** later | "For agencies" page and niche landing pages |

**Case studies need the most rework.** The standard structure is Problem → Process → Results, kept to 300–800 words, with specific numbers or before/after evidence and the client's own words ([SolidGigs](https://solidgigs.com/blog/?p=2259); [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-write-a-case-study/)). Developers disagree on whether clients care about the stack ([DEV](https://dev.to/hzzhdev/nobody-cares-about-your-stack-and-your-code-quality-3lfl) vs [DEV](https://dev.to/codingnninja/clients-care-about-the-stack-you-use-g2g)). The workable middle ground is to show it as a secondary line that names the benefit, for example "Astro: near-instant loads for image-heavy galleries." `projects.ts` already has `problem`, `approach`, `highlights` and `outcome` fields. Two additions are needed: a `results` field (Lighthouse/Core Web Vitals scores, load times, launch date, what the client can now do themselves) and a `testimonial` field (name, role, business, photo). The current copy is written for engineers. Phrases like "useSyncExternalStore on top of localStorage" belong in the repo README, not on the client-facing card.

**Be honest about what each project is.** That honesty also builds trust. JD Gallery is the only public, commissioned client site, so it should lead. Ask Dan for a testimonial now and offer to draft it for his approval ([Her Agenda](https://heragenda.com/p/13-ways-to-make-your-unpaid-project-pay-off)). Phil Strats is a self-initiated content site built to show work that a private freelance client cannot share. Label it as an own project and pitch it on content-site outcomes: structured SEO data, newsletter, analytics, and content that is easy to keep adding. The jewelry store is explicitly a concept build, so label it "Concept build, in progress" and show design and performance proof rather than invented sales metrics. AIGA's warnings about spec work ([AIGA](https://AIGA.org/resources/aiga-position-on-spec-work)) and plain honesty point the same way: never imply a client relationship that doesn't exist.

**Replace the bare contact form with qualify-then-book.** Sources recommend a short intake form with project type, a **budget range** (people skip an exact-figure field), and a timeline. Show a Cal.com or Calendly link only to qualified leads, because an open booking link lets anyone take your time ([ExpertFreelancing](https://expertfreelancing.com/typeform-vs-tally-freelancer-intake-forms); [splitforms](https://splitforms.com/blog/best-contact-form-for-consultants)). The current EmailJS form sends only `name`, `email` and `message`, and the EmailJS template reads those exact field names. Any new fields must be added to the template at the same time. Suggested budget bands are under $1k, $1–3k, $3–6k and $6k+. Change the CTA label from "Contact" to "Book a free 20-minute project call" or "Get a quote."

**Spell out trust signals, because remote clients cannot meet you.** Clients who can't meet a freelancer rely on digital cues. Stating response times, milestones and revision limits up front reduces friction ([Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/business/how-to-build-trust-with-overseas-clients-as-a-freelancer)). Testimonials should carry a full name, role, business, photo and date ([Inkbot Design](https://inkbotdesign.com/social-proof-marketing/)). Place them on case-study and services pages, where buyers are evaluating, and not only on the homepage ([LowCode Agency](https://www.lowcode.agency/blog/b2b-website-social-proof-how-to-use-it-strategically)). A good trust line reads: "Based in the Philippines (UTC+8): live overlap with Australian business hours, UK mornings in my afternoon, and overnight turnaround for US clients. Replies within one business day."

## Price fixed packages between offshore floors and onshore rates

Benchmarks frame the range. Onshore freelance web developers charge about **US$50–100+/hr** in the US. In the UK the figures are **£250–600/day**, with a median of about £431/day ([osdire](https://osdire.com/blog/web-developer-rates-in-the-uk-hourly-daily-and-project-costs); [Wise UK](https://wise.com/gb/blog/how-much-to-charge-as-a-freelance-web-developer)). In Australia a modelled estimate gives **A$90–150/hr** for Shopify developers ([projectcostestimator](https://projectcostestimator.com/cost/shopify/australia)). Upwork lists web developers at **$15–50/hr** ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/web-developers/cost/)). A hiring firm puts mid-level Philippine React freelancers at **$25–40/hr** ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/developer-rate-card/web-developer-philippines/)), but that is a marketing rate card, and no independent PH survey newer than 2022 was found. Per project, a freelancer-built 5-page brochure site typically costs **$1,500–4,000** ([dev.to](https://dev.to/sleeksky/how-much-should-a-small-business-website-cost-in-2026-ofn)). Real listings start as low as **$900** ([Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/NextUpStudioATX/services)). Most Australian businesses pay **A$2,000–8,000** for a template-based Shopify store ([whatsthedamage.com.au](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/shopify-cost/)). Care plans run **$29–299/month** across typical tiers ([Seahawk](https://seahawkmedia.com/wordpress/wordpress-maintenance-packages/)), and one freelancer charges $89/month ([cre8d](https://www.cre8d-design.com/services/care-plans/)).

**Productized fixed-price packages** suit this work. They have a defined scope, revision rounds and a timeline, plus a deposit to book ([Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/productized-services-for-freelance-designers/)), and international buyers can compare them without a call. Public pricing has trade-offs. Publishing prices filters out poor-fit leads and shortens sales calls ([Speckyboy](https://speckyboy.com/should-freelancers-advertise-their-pricing/)). On the downside, clients can fixate on the low figure, and raising rates gets harder ([Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/should-web-designer-list-prices); [Millo](https://millo.co/should-i-list-my-prices-on-my-website)). For async overseas buyers, "starting at" prices tied to a clearly defined base package are the best compromise. The ladder below is my synthesis, placed below onshore freelancer prices but above the bargain floor. The figures are recommendations, not sourced market rates.

| Package | What's included (define in writing) | Suggested "starting at" | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid site audit (entry offer) | Performance, SEO, accessibility and copy review with a Loom walkthrough; fee credited toward a build | US$150–300 | 3–5 days |
| Launch / portfolio site | 1–3 pages, copy polish, responsive, basic SEO, launch and DNS | US$1,200 | 2 weeks |
| Business site | Up to 5–6 pages, copywriting, CMS/blog, analytics, SEO setup | US$2,000 | 3–5 weeks |
| Content site | Astro/Next or WordPress with CMS, newsletter, structured data, migration | US$2,400 | 4–6 weeks |
| Shop | Small Shopify or WooCommerce store on a theme, product setup, checkout testing | US$3,000 | 4–6 weeks |
| Care plans | Updates, backups, uptime, monthly report; higher tier adds edit hours | US$79 / US$149 per month | Ongoing |

Two items in this ladder need comment. The research found **no demand data for Shopify specifically**, but most small brands sell on Shopify, so "Shopify store" will likely sell better than "custom Next.js + Supabase commerce." Keep the custom stack as the proof of engineering depth behind the offer. Bundling the first one to three months of care into each build starts recurring revenue. The freelancer-specific risk with care plans is availability: if you are offline when a site goes down, the client waits ([Freshy Sites](https://freshysites.com/blog/how-much-should-you-pay-for-wordpress-maintenance-services-a-pricing-guide/)). Publish the response window, not 24/7 support. Use the effective rates (US$25–45/hr) only to quote out-of-scope change requests.

**Contracts protect more than price does.** The industry standard is **50% upfront, 50% before launch**, or 50/25/25 on larger jobs with the middle payment at design approval ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/NAAvZWNm-web-design-payment-schedules-a-guide-to-deposits-and-milestones)). Say explicitly whether the deposit is refundable, because a bare "50% upfront" invites disputes ([Sprintlaw AU](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/web-design-payment-milestones/)). The contract should set a fixed number of revision rounds, with written change orders beyond them ([Elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/)). It also needs a kill fee of 25–50% of the remaining balance ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/web-design-contract)), IP transfer only on final payment, client-owned hosting and domain accounts, a short bug-fix warranty, and governing law. Australian Consumer Law can override unfair deposit terms ([Sprintlaw AU](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/refund-and-cancellation-terms-for-web-design-agencies-in-australia/)), so don't rely on harsh clauses. Cross-border debt collection is impractical, so the deposit is the real protection. Don't pick AND.CO (Fiverr Workspace) as a contract tool: Fiverr says it shut down on 1 March 2026 ([Fiverr help](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/fr/articles/4410092851345-Fiverr-Workspace)).

## Get paid through Wise and register with the BIR early

| Method | Cost to receive (third-party figures) | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| **Wise** | Free to receive USD/GBP via local account details; one-time ₱1,400 to unlock them ([Wise PH](https://wise.com/ph/blog/wise-vs-payoneer)) | Default for invoices; give US clients USD details, UK clients GBP details |
| **Payoneer** | Up to 1% on receipts; $29.95/yr if under $6k received; up to 3.99% on cards ([Wise PH](https://wise.com/ph/blog/payoneer-fees)) | Card payments; marketplace payouts |
| **PayPal** | ~4.4% + fixed fee internationally, plus 3–4% currency conversion; free withdrawal to GCash ([Wise PH](https://wise.com/ph/blog/receive-money-paypal-philippines)) | Clients who insist on it; build the fee into the price |
| **Stripe** | Does not directly support PH-registered businesses ([Wise PH](https://wise.com/ph/blog/stripe-vs-paypal); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/payments-in-the-philippines)) | Not available; don't plan checkout around it |

The Wise fee figures come largely from Wise's own comparison pages, which are competitor-biased, and the official PayPal and Payoneer fee pages could not be retrieved. Check live pricing before relying on these numbers. The site's FAQ should say "Invoices in USD, AUD or GBP; pay by bank transfer or card." That removes one more reason a foreign client might hesitate.

On tax, a Philippine resident is taxed on worldwide income and must register with the BIR as self-employed. Sources disagree on one point. Most say a freelancer with gross receipts **at or under ₱3M** (roughly US$51k at an unverified ~₱58/US$) who is not VAT-registered can elect an **8% tax on gross receipts above ₱250,000**, in place of graduated rates and the 3% percentage tax. The election is made on the first quarterly 1701Q and is irrevocable for that year ([Wise PH](https://wise.com/ph/blog/freelance-tax-philippines); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/freelancer-tax-filing-in-the-philippines); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/philippines-bir-freelancer-influencer-tax-2026-guide)). On ₱1.2M of receipts that works out to **₱76,000**, an effective rate of about 6.3%. The ₱500 annual registration fee was abolished from **22 January 2024** ([PwC PH](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-02.html)). Registration now involves a ₱30 documentary stamp tax and registered books of accounts ([PhilstarLife](https://philstarlife.com/self/848990-how-to-register-freelancer-bir-philippines-2026)). Under the EOPT Act, **you issue an invoice, not an official receipt**, for every payment. An OR issued after 30 June 2024 is treated as a failure to invoice ([PwC PH](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2024/eopt-invoicing-clarified.html)). Mandatory e-invoicing takes effect on **31 December 2026** under RR 26-2025 and RMC 98-2026. The covered e-commerce taxpayers are classified Small, Medium or Large, and **Micro taxpayers are exempted** ([Grant Thornton PH](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2026/bir-issues-guidelines-on-electronic-invoicing-retains-31-dec-2026-deadline/); [PwC PH](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2025/tax-alert-29.html)). A new freelancer under ₱3M appears to be exempt. That conclusion, the filing deadlines, and the VAT treatment above ₱3M (foreign-currency services may qualify for 0%) all need confirmation from a tax adviser, because no BIR primary documents were retrieved.

## Win first clients through warm networks and agencies

No source ranks channels rigorously, but the evidence leans one way. Freelancermap's 2025 study found **58%** of freelancers name client acquisition as their main obstacle, and **62%** get projects passively, mostly through referrals ([freelancermap](https://www.freelancermap.com/freelancer-tips/7816-how-to-get-referrals-by-earning-them-instead-of-asking)). An informal 2026 poll of developers found the most common route to a first paying client was a direct, specific ask to one's own network, or a **small paid audit** rather than free work ([doBoard](https://www.doboard.com/?p=5556)). A WPMU DEV survey found only **9%** of WordPress developers got started through sites like Upwork ([WPMU DEV](https://wpmudev.com/blog/?p=211808)). As one practitioner put it, "create a profile and wait. That doesn't work" ([Hostinger PH](https://www.hostinger.com/ph/tutorials/get-clients-as-freelance-developer)).

The agency web-support job is the most underused asset. Agencies outsource because "agency pipelines are lumpy" ([Codeable](https://www.codeable.io/blog/white-label-web-development/)). They vet white-label partners on "trust and process, not price alone" ([Seahawk](https://seahawkmedia.com/agency-infrastructure/hiring-white-label-partner/)) and want stack-specific portfolio examples and references ([The White Label Agency](https://thewhitelabelagency.com/?p=5584)). These sources are mostly vendors selling white-label services, but the logic holds: agencies are repeat buyers, Philippine hours give them overnight capacity, and experience with launches, DNS and migrations shows you know agency workflows. That justifies a **"For agencies" page** covering Figma-to-code in Next, Astro or WordPress, maintenance, speed and accessibility fixes, the QA and handoff process, overlap hours, NDA willingness and rates. Ask the former employer for overflow work and a reference first.

Audit-style cold outreach comes next. A freelance developer who sent 225 cold emails reported an **8% reply rate**, and the variant with a free 60-second Loom audit booked all four meetings ([DEV](https://dev.to/gentleaffair274/i-sent-225-cold-emails-in-one-day-here-are-the-numbers-1hd5)). A guide aimed at web designers estimates **4–6%** replies for emails that point out two or three specific issues on the prospect's site ([PuzzleInbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-for-web-design-agencies)). The results are mixed: one practitioner's replies *rose* after they dropped Looms ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/lead-gen/i-stopped-sending-looms-replies-went-up?p=7d61c9d8)), and small businesses often lack budget or have a relative "doing their website" ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)). Expect single-digit reply rates. Treat this as a volume game aimed at the audience cluster: Australian photographers and small jewelry and lifestyle shops whose sites are slow or built on DIY builders.

Marketplaces are a paid supplement. Upwork raised per-proposal costs to 1–6 Connects ([Time Doctor](https://www.timedoctor.com/blog/?p=15825)) and moved new contracts to a variable **0–15%** service fee from May 2025 ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-fees)). One summary of Upwork's own page conflicts with that figure, so verify it. Coding jobs on Upwork fell 20% between 2021 and 2023 ([People Matters SEA](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/article/organisational-culture/filipino-freelancers-on-upwork-find-themselves-competing-with-ai-45311)). If you use Upwork, set a fixed monthly Connects budget, target jobs with few proposals, and link each proposal to the matching case-study page rather than the homepage. Contra takes no commission and is cheap to keep current ([Contra](https://contra.com/commission-free)). Fiverr takes 20% ([Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/popular-freelance-marketplaces/)). Toptal accepts fewer than 3% of applicants after weeks of screening ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/top-3-percent)), so it is not an entry point. Content SEO for the site itself is a long-term bet. Location-based SEO ("web designer in [city]") works for locals but not for someone outside the target cities ([SolidGigs](https://solidgigs.com/blog/6-proven-ways-to-promote-your-portfolio-website-and-drive-organic-traffic/)). Niche posts such as "Astro for photographers" or a WordPress-to-Astro migration guide are more realistic, but no source puts a number on their lead yield.

## Prioritized rebuild plan

The sequence follows one principle: fix what a referred or pitched prospect sees in the first 30 seconds, then add the pages that outreach channels link to. All edits map to existing files. Most early changes are content edits in `src/data/` rather than new architecture.

| Priority | Change | Where in the repo | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|

## Conclusion

The rebrand is less a redesign than a change in who the site is talking to. The portfolio already contains the raw material a client site needs: a commissioned photographer site, a content site with real SEO engineering, a commerce build, and agency launch experience. All of it is currently written for an engineering hiring manager. The work is mostly translation: from stack to outcome, from timeline to process, from "open to work" to "here is what it costs and how we start." The less obvious lesson from the research is that the site cannot carry the business alone. Its value depends on the outreach that sends people to it, so pages like "For agencies," the sample audit and per-project case studies are distribution tools, not decoration.

Two strategic bets are worth testing rather than assuming. The first is whether the creative and lifestyle audience cluster or white-label agency work produces paying clients faster. The agency route plays to the existing background and brings repeat volume. The direct-client route builds the brand and pays more per project. The second is the Shopify question: the research suggests small brands buy Shopify, but there is no hard demand data, so the first ten conversations should settle it. Review pricing after three to five projects. The suggested starting points sit deliberately below onshore rates, and the Filipino freelancers who escape the low-rate anchor are the ones who raise prices as soon as testimonials justify it.
