# Freelance (client-facing) developer site structure vs. a hiring portfolio

> Method note: WebFetch could not reach most hosts in this session (paigebrunton.com, namecheap.com, jonathanstark.com, dev.to and codestitch.app all failed DNS because of the session's network allowlist). Most findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads, so the report writer should treat exact wording as approximate. Most sources are vendor or freelancer blogs (opinion, not controlled data). No Nielsen Norman Group, Baymard or CXL research specific to freelancer sites turned up, and no Reddit threads came back from searches.

## What sections/pages do high-converting freelance developer / small studio sites include, and in what order?

### Takeaway
The recurring blueprint is a niche-specific value-prop hero, then services (often with "starting at" prices), 3–6 outcome-focused case studies, testimonials, a short about section with a photo, and a prominent "Book a call" / inquiry CTA. Positioning (who you serve and what result you deliver) matters more than layout. The site mostly works as a credibility check for referrals and outreach, not as a lead source by itself.

### Cited Findings
- Recommended core set: a clear headline, 3–6 detailed case studies, services (ideally with starting prices), testimonials, a short about page and an easy way to contact you — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)
- The homepage should work as a pitch: who you serve, what you do, and why you are different. A specific headline beats a job title (example: "Brand identities for independent coffee shops" beats "Graphic designer") — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)
- Case studies should cover the client's goals, the process, key features and measurable results. The site also needs a clear services page, client testimonials and a short introduction — [Wix blog, freelance website examples](https://www.wix.com/blog/freelance-website-examples)
- A visible "Book a Call"–style CTA, testimonials and a photo of yourself are recommended because clients want to see who they are hiring — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/); [Sternhost 2026](https://sternhost.com/freelancer-portfolio-website/)
- Positioning advice: Jonathan Stark's homepage leads with a niche positioning line ("I help consultants and professional service firms price their services more profitably"). His teaching says the fastest way to get more leads is to make your positioning "laser focused" and put it everywhere strangers will see you — [jonathanstark.com](https://jonathanstark.com/); [Ditching Hourly podcast](https://share.transistor.fm/s/1292876c)
- A specialty can be repeated in the headline, copy, about section and homepage (Freelancers Union, 2019, older) — [Freelancers Union blog](https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2019/11/21/why-these-freelance-website-examples-work/)
- Brennan Dunn's homepage is built around a lead magnet (a free email course) rather than an immediate "hire me" CTA — [WP Engine examples](https://wpengine.com/resources/freelance-business-websites-inspiring-examples/)
- A survey of 3,424 independents reportedly found only 4% of projects came through the freelancer's own website. This suggests the site works mainly as a credibility check for referrals, not as a lead generator (secondhand; primary survey not verified) — [Namecheap blog](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/why-freelancer-websites-fail-and-how-to-fix-yours/)
- Some guides recommend niche blog articles for long-term search traffic, and a Google Business Profile for local clients — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)
- Studio sites often present a numbered process (for example, an agency with a "six-step process") — [Colorlib studio examples](https://colorlib.com/wp/web-design-studio-website-examples/)

### Inferences
- A sensible order, synthesized from the sources (no tested benchmark was found): Hero (who you help + outcome + primary CTA) → proof strip (logos, a quote or a result) → Services/packages → Selected case studies → Process → Testimonials → About (photo, location/timezone) → FAQ → Final CTA/booking. Repeat the CTA after the hero, after case studies and in the footer.
- Because only a small share of work comes from the website itself, the site should be optimized for "a referred or cold-pitched prospect checks you out." The priorities are fast credibility, clear offer and an easy next step.
- For this person, a niche angle drawn from existing work could be "sites for small e-commerce brands, travel/hospitality, and creatives/photographers." That is stronger than a generic "frontend developer."

### Gaps
- No controlled conversion data (CXL/NNG/Baymard) on section order for freelancer sites specifically.

## How should case studies be reframed for clients? Should tech stacks be shown?

### Takeaway
The consensus is Problem → Process/Solution → Results, short (300–800 words), with specific numbers or before/after evidence and the client's own words. The tech stack should be a secondary detail, explained in benefit terms (speed, easy editing, cost) rather than as a logo wall.

### Cited Findings
- The standard arc is a three-part Problem, Process, Results structure. A typical outline adds headline → overview → challenges → solution → results — [SolidGigs](https://solidgigs.com/blog/?p=2259); [selfemployed.com case study guide](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-write-a-case-study/)
- Suggested length is 300–800 words so a busy client can skim it; another guide recommends keeping it under a page — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-write-a-case-study/); [The Write Life](https://thewritelife.com/case-study-freelance-writer/)
- The more specific the results, the better. Where no hard metrics exist, focus on process and qualitative outcomes such as client satisfaction and before/after comparisons — [Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/how-to-write-a-freelance-case-study-that-wins-clients/); [9am.works](https://www.9am.works/freelancer-academy/blog/freelance-case-studies-portfolio-that-wins-projects)
- Involve the client: send a short email with a few questions to collect their words and permission. Check NDAs, and consider anonymous case studies if needed — [SolidGigs](https://solidgigs.com/blog/?p=2259)
- Each project should be paired with a specific outcome number (problem → action → measurable result) — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)
- On the tech stack, opinions differ. One view is "the business does not care that you chose tech X instead of Y for technical reason Z" — [DEV: Nobody cares about your stack](https://dev.to/hzzhdev/nobody-cares-about-your-stack-and-your-code-quality-3lfl). The counter-view is that clients do care about the stack if you explain its benefits in terms they understand — [DEV: Clients care about the stack you use](https://dev.to/codingnninja/clients-care-about-the-stack-you-use-g2g)
- "Amateurs stack tech. Pros stack value." Frame projects around the problem solved and the impact — [Substack: Full-stack developers who win](https://jeniferrajendren.substack.com/p/full-stack-developers-who-win-its)
- For beginners without client work, realistic mock projects (for example, a landing page for a fictional startup) are acceptable — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)

### Inferences
- Case study template for this person's projects: Client/industry → the business problem (for example, "a jewelry brand needed to sell online without Shopify fees") → what was built and the key decisions → results (Lighthouse/Core Web Vitals scores, load time, launch date, traffic or bookings if available, what the client can now do themselves) → client quote → a short "Built with" line with benefit-framed stack notes (for example, "Astro → near-instant page loads for image-heavy galleries").
- For the in-progress jewelry store, the honest framing is "in progress / build log" with design and performance proof rather than invented metrics.
- Mini/fun projects should be demoted to a "Lab/Playground" link, or used only where they show a client-relevant skill (interactions, animation).

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence that hiding or showing stacks changes client conversion.

## What CTAs and contact mechanisms convert best?

### Takeaway
Sources mostly recommend a short qualifying inquiry form (project type, budget range, timeline) and then a booking link (Calendly/Cal.com) for qualified leads, over either a bare email or an unscreened calendar link. A visible "Book a call" CTA is still standard on the page.

### Cited Findings
- A booking link with no intake lets anyone reserve your time. Send the booking link only to qualified submissions, in the auto-reply or a follow-up — [ExpertFreelancing: intake forms](https://expertfreelancing.com/typeform-vs-tally-freelancer-intake-forms)
- Conditional routing: if the budget is below your minimum, thank them and suggest alternatives. If the budget qualifies and the timeline isn't "just exploring," show the booking link — [ExpertFreelancing](https://expertfreelancing.com/typeform-vs-tally-freelancer-intake-forms)
- The budget field is the one people skip most, so ask for a range rather than an exact figure. It saves wasted discovery calls — [splitforms: contact forms for consultants](https://splitforms.com/blog/best-contact-form-for-consultants)
- Calendly routing forms screen people before booking and send them to different event types. A community post warns that the routing logic can still let mismatched leads through — [Calendly blog](https://calendly.com/blog/routing-forms.html); [Calendly community](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/optimizing-calendly-routing-forms-for-effective-lead-qualification-in-consulting-4241)
- Example web designer inquiry form templates exist with budget and timeline fields — [Paperform template](https://paperform.co/templates/freelance-web-designer-project-inquiry-form)
- A HoneyBook lead-form guide (vendor) covers contact-form best practices — [HoneyBook](https://honeybook.com/blog/best-practices-contact-form)

### Inferences
- For this site, the existing EmailJS form could be extended with project type, a budget range dropdown (for example, <$1k / $1–3k / $3–6k / $6k+), timeline and a "how did you hear about me" field. The success state should show a Cal.com/Calendly link. Keep a plain email address visible for people who prefer it.
- The primary CTA label should be outcome-oriented ("Book a free 20-min project call" / "Get a quote") rather than "Contact."

### Gaps
- No independent A/B data comparing form-first vs. calendar-first for freelancers; all sources are vendors or bloggers.

## Should prices be shown publicly ("starting at")?

### Takeaway
There is no consensus. The common middle ground is "starting at" prices tied to clearly defined base packages, which filters out poor-fit leads and shortens sales calls. The trade-off is anchoring on the low number and less flexibility to raise rates.

### Cited Findings
- For: publishing prices filters out poor-fit clients. A freelancer who added starting prices reported fewer ghost inquiries, better-fit clients and shorter sales calls — [Speckyboy](https://speckyboy.com/should-freelancers-advertise-their-pricing/); [DEV: pricing transparency](https://dev.to/imtaslim/why-freelancers-should-display-pricing-transparency-on-portfolios-2k60)
- Against: posted prices can set wrong expectations when scope varies, make raising rates harder (you must do it across the board), and expose you to undercutting — [Speckyboy](https://speckyboy.com/should-freelancers-advertise-their-pricing/); [Freelancers Union, 2022](https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2022/07/18/should-freelancers-make-their-prices-public/); [Millo](https://millo.co/should-i-list-my-prices-on-my-website)
- "Starting at" should name the most basic project and spell out what that base package includes. The risk is that clients fixate on the low figure and any higher quote sounds expensive — [Medium: Should you show your prices](https://medium.com/the-next-leap/should-you-show-your-prices-on-your-website-or-not-4fbc29e5c24b); [Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/should-web-designer-list-prices)
- Listing a starting price is repeatedly recommended to screen out poor-fit leads — [selfemployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/how-to-build-a-freelance-website-that-wins-clients/)
- Productized services: the client sees the deliverable, price and timeline before talking to you. Scope boundaries are critical ("a package with soft scope is still soft") — [Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/productized-services-for-freelance-designers/); [Elegant Themes: reasons for and against packages](https://elegantthemes.com/blog/tips-tricks/3-reasons-to-offer-web-design-packages-and-3-reasons-not-to)
- Example tiers on Contra: Landing from $1,500 / 2 weeks; Personal from $2,500 / 3–4 weeks; Business from $4,500 / 6–8 weeks. Clutch package listings include "starting at $2,500" and "$500/mo." There is a subscription model at $599/month (ManyPixels) and a $2,500 fixed fee with a 50% deposit (Twine example). These are examples, not market averages — [Contra](https://contra.com/s/R2kBlEUU-website-design-and-development-custom-packages); [Clutch packages](https://clutch.co/web-designers/packages?page=12); [ManyPixels](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/web-design/website-design-packages); [Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/productized-services-for-freelance-designers/)
- Suggested payment terms: 100% upfront for small packages, 50/50 for larger ones — [Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/productized-services-for-freelance-designers/)

### Inferences
- For a new freelancer targeting US/AU/UK small businesses, 3 "starting at" packages (for example, Landing page / Small business site / E-commerce build) plus a care/maintenance retainer, with a "custom quote" option, fit the evidence. The packages should define page count, revisions, timeline and what's excluded.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data on conversion impact of public pricing; all evidence is anecdotal.
- No data on what price points US/AU/UK clients expect from Philippines-based developers (other researchers may cover pricing).

## What trust signals matter when the client has never met you?

### Takeaway
Specific, attributed testimonials placed next to the work, a real photo, a clear step-by-step process, explicit response-time and communication norms, stated timezone and working-hour overlap, milestones/contracts/deposits, and a project count. Remote/offshore buyers lean heavily on these because they can't evaluate you in person.

### Cited Findings
- Clients who can't meet a freelancer rely on digital cues, and a polished online presence settles doubts before the first call. Stating response-time expectations, milestones and revision limits up front reduces friction. Reliable deadlines carry extra weight across borders — [Remitly](https://www.remitly.com/blog/business/how-to-build-trust-with-overseas-clients-as-a-freelancer)
- Published response-time guidelines help, because clients may worry about gaps in replies — [Upwork: time zones](https://www.upwork.com/resources/client-communication-across-time-zones)
- One vendor suggests at least ~4 hours of daily overlap. Asia-based teams may have only 1–2 hours of overlap with US hours. A developer many hours ahead can turn work around overnight, since requests arrive before their day starts — [doit.software](https://doit.software/?p=20814); [Codeable](https://www.codeable.io/guide-working-developers-different-time-zones/)
- Measuring response time in hours rather than days is a recommended vetting question — [Full Scale](https://fullscale.io/blog/how-to-choose-offshore-development-partner)
- Onshore competitors pitch native English, shared culture and real-time turnaround as advantages (commercially biased source) — [Productive Machine](https://productivemachine.com/10-good-reasons-to-keep-your-wordpress-development-onshore/)
- Testimonials should carry full credibility markers: full name, title, company, headshot or logo, and date — [Inkbot Design](https://inkbotdesign.com/social-proof-marketing/)
- Social proof should be specific, placed where buyers are evaluating (case study pages, services pages, not just the homepage), and matched to target buyers — [LowCode Agency](https://www.lowcode.agency/blog/b2b-website-social-proof-how-to-use-it-strategically)
- Volume signals (for example, "X projects delivered") help when individual references are thin — [LowCode Agency trust signals](https://www.lowcode.agency/blog/b2b-website-trust-signals)
- Nielsen's (the ratings firm, not NN/g) trust research ranks recommendations from people you know first and consumer opinions/testimonials near the top — [Zoho Academy](https://www.zoho.com/academy/testimonials/introducing-customer-testimonials.html); see also [CXL on testimonials](https://cxl.com/how-why-you-should-invest-in-getting-good-testimonials-w-examples/)
- Many viral trust-lift statistics can't be traced to primary sources (for example, "34% higher trust" for lessons-learned case studies) — [LowCode Agency](https://www.lowcode.agency/blog/b2b-website-trust-signals)

### Inferences
- Philippines-specific trust copy could read: "Based in Manila (GMT+8). Overlap with AU business hours, morning calls with US East Coast/UK evenings, overnight turnaround for US clients. Replies within 24h on weekdays." Add a fixed-price contract, deposit + milestone payments, and payment via Wise/PayPal/Stripe invoices. Note English fluency and async tools (Loom updates, shared Trello/Notion board).
- A FAQ section is the natural home for objections: timezone, payment, ownership of code/domain/hosting, post-launch support, and editing content yourself.

### Gaps
- No independent survey on how US/AU/UK clients weigh offshore-freelancer trust signals; the sources are agencies and marketplaces with a stake.
- No evidence found on guarantees (for example, money-back) for freelance web devs specifically.

## What should be removed or demoted from a job-seeking portfolio?

### Takeaway
Clients ask "what have you done for people like me," not for a résumé. Résumé downloads, "open to work" badges, employment timelines and skills/logo marquees serve recruiters. On a client site they should be dropped or moved to a secondary page, and side projects reframed or tucked into a playground.

### Cited Findings
- Prospective clients usually ask what you've done for people like them and seldom request a résumé. A portfolio website is better than a résumé when pitching services directly to clients — [Millo: freelancer resume](https://millo.co/freelancer-resume); [Indy](https://weareindy.com/blog/does-every-freelancer-need-a-website-how-to-build-your-portfolio-website)
- Résumés remain relevant for employer and ATS hiring, so keep one available if you still apply for jobs — [Prezumi](https://www.prezumi.com/blog/portfolio-vs-resume); [proveyou.to](https://proveyou.to/blog/resume-vs-portfolio-website-what-recruiters-look-at)
- Business buyers don't care about tech choices for their own sake (see stack findings above) — [DEV](https://dev.to/hzzhdev/nobody-cares-about-your-stack-and-your-code-quality-3lfl)

### Inferences (applied to the current site's components, per CLAUDE.md)
- Remove: the `openToWork` badge and résumé download (`resumeUrl`) from the hero and nav. Move the résumé to an unlinked or footer-only URL if job applications continue.
- Demote or replace: the `Skills` marquee → a short "Tools I use" line or benefit-framed tech notes inside services and case studies. `Experience` timeline → a one-paragraph credibility line on About ("X years building for…"), or remove it. `Challenges` section → "Lab"/"Playground" page, kept off the homepage or near the bottom.
- Add: Services/Packages, Process, Testimonials, FAQ and a qualified booking CTA. Reframe `projects.ts` case-study fields as problem/solution/results/quote.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on whether showing job history hurts client conversion; this is expert opinion and inference.
